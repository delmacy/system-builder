## 3. Target decision

**DECISION C3.27-D1 — establish a provider-neutral, revision-qualified Technology Economic Governance Plane whose canonical identities, normalization, allocation, economic models and evidence remain reproducible across provider changes, late corrections and organizational evolution.**

The plane has thirteen linked semantic surfaces:

1. **Economic Source Evidence Plane** — qualified provider/on-prem/internal economic source evidence with provenance/currentness.
2. **Normalization Plane** — canonical economic dimensions, quantity/unit/currency/time normalization and source coverage.
3. **Technology Economic Identity Plane** — provider-neutral resource/service/cost-pool/economic-account identities and mappings.
4. **Allocation Plane** — direct/shared/unallocated cost distribution under immutable policy revisions with conservation.
5. **Rate & Cost Model Plane** — internal rates, transfer-cost models and model lineage distinct from customer pricing.
6. **Budget / Forecast / Commitment Plane** — distinct economic intentions/exposure classes and immutable revisions.
7. **Unit Economics Plane** — metric definitions, numerator/denominator provenance and revision-qualified results.
8. **Showback / Internal Chargeback Plane** — internal accountability evidence without customer invoice or statutory posting authority.
9. **Relative Operational Complexity Plane** — bounded, multidimensional complexity evidence derived from auditable architecture/operations facts.
10. **Provider / Evolution Plane** — source qualification, substitution/coexistence, late evidence and residual economic cohorts.
11. **Federation / Local / Fleet Plane** — local truth, aggregation, offline/delayed evidence and qualified economic closure.
12. **Reconciliation / Correction Plane** — late credits, rebills, corrections, supersession and historical reproduction.
13. **Governance / Elicitation / Readiness Plane** — ownership, audit, privacy, operational elicitation and multidimensional readiness.

## 5. Qualified economic evidence and normalization

**DECISION C3.27-D3 — provider invoices, cost-and-usage exports, observability-derived utilization and on-prem measurements are evidence sources; normalized economic truth requires explicit qualification.**

Each source occurrence carries at least:

- source/provider identity and revision;
- provider account/subaccount/project/resource coordinates;
- evidence interval and observation/ingestion timestamps;
- quantity and original unit;
- monetary amount and original currency;
- cost type/category semantics where provided;
- invoice/billing-period linkage where available;
- tags/labels/metadata with source provenance;
- completeness/currentness status;
- correction/supersession lineage;
- source support vector and known exclusions.

Normalization converts source evidence into canonical dimensions without erasing originals. Missing or stale evidence is never silently coerced to zero. Conflicting provider and local evidence yields `PARTIAL` or `INCONCLUSIVE` until governed disposition.

External normalization standards such as FOCUS may be used as interoperable realizations. As of June 4, 2026, FOCUS 1.4 includes invoice-detail and billing-period datasets, commitment detail and standardized correction/completeness semantics. This is useful evidence for portability, not canonical SB authority.

## 6. Currency, units, time and calculation provenance

**DECISION C3.27-D4 — every economic value is typed by currency/unit/time semantics and every derived value is reproducible from a revision vector.**

An economic calculation records:

- source evidence identities and revisions;
- original and normalized units;
- currency and conversion source/effective time if conversion is used;
- billing/usage/observation windows;
- normalization, allocation, rate and model revisions;
- rounding points/modes and precision;
- organization/resource mapping revisions;
- correction/supersession lineage;
- resulting amount/quantity and confidence/qualification state.

Historical reproduction uses the producing revision vector. Current-policy simulation is a separate labeled computation.

`historical rate != current applicable rate` and `historical normalized cost != current-policy rerating`.

## 7. Direct cost, shared cost, allocation policy and conservation

**DECISION C3.27-D5 — allocation is a transparent policy transformation over qualified economic evidence; it never becomes proof of causality.**

Allocation policies explicitly classify cost into direct, shared, residual/unallocated and excluded populations. Shared-cost strategies may use fixed shares, equal split, proportional consumption, capacity reservation, organizational weights or other approved mechanics, each with explicit denominator source and applicability window.

For every closed allocation population, conservation is mandatory within declared currency/rounding tolerance:

`source amount = allocated amount + residual/unallocated amount + explicitly excluded adjustment`

No algorithm may hide residuals to force 100% apparent attribution. Rounding residuals remain explicit.

Allocation also preserves:

`allocated-to X != caused-by X`.

FinOps Foundation guidance independently distinguishes direct and shared allocation and recommends explicit shared-cost strategy; this target extracts that portable semantic without adopting Foundation maturity scores as architecture authority.

## 8. Internal rates and cost models

**DECISION C3.27-D6 — internal rates and cost models are immutable, effective-dated economic policies distinct from provider list price, provider billed rate, customer price and statutory accounting rate.**

A rate/model can compose:

- normalized provider cost;
- internal labor/support/platform cost evidence;
- amortization or depreciation-derived inputs supplied by an external accounting owner;
- reserved/committed capacity economics;
- shared overhead allocation;
- utilization/capacity denominator evidence;
- risk/headroom or policy adjustments where explicitly governed;
- currency/unit conversion.

The result is an internal economic fact with calculation provenance, not a customer-commercial price. C3.26 remains owner of customer-facing plan/price/charge semantics.

## 9. Budget, forecast, commitment and actual are distinct

**DECISION C3.27-D7 — budget, forecast, commitment exposure and actual cost are separate revisioned semantic objects and must never be merged into one mutable `spend` number.**

- **Budget** expresses authorized/planned economic envelope according to owning governance.
- **Forecast** predicts future outcomes with model/input lineage and uncertainty.
- **Commitment exposure** captures contract/reservation/minimum-spend obligations and benefit eligibility/coverage.
- **Actual** derives from qualified realized economic evidence.

Variance is a derived comparison whose operands, periods and revision vectors remain visible.

A forecast may exceed budget without being an authorization change. A commitment may exist without actual utilization. Provider-applied commitment benefit and internal allocation of that benefit are separate evidence transformations.

## 10. Unit economics

**DECISION C3.27-D8 — unit economics is defined by revisioned numerator and denominator semantics, not by dividing arbitrary dashboard totals.**

A `UnitEconomicMetricDefinitionRevision` declares:

- cost numerator definition and included/excluded populations;
- denominator semantic owner and evidence source;
- unit and aggregation window;
- allocation/rate/model revisions;
- applicability scope;
- handling of missing/late evidence;
- currentness and confidence requirements.

Examples may include cost per workflow completion, tenant, active user, API request, GB-month, build, deployment, supported Station or other business/technical unit. The denominator owner remains the capability that owns the underlying semantic fact.

`unit cost = normalized qualified cost / qualified denominator`

only when both populations/windows are compatible and denominator validity is proven.

## 11. Showback, internal chargeback and customer-commercial separation

**DECISION C3.27-D9 — showback and internal chargeback are internal accountability artifacts and must not be confused with customer invoices or statutory postings.**

Showback exposes qualified allocated technology costs to responsible internal consumers without necessarily generating accounting postings. Internal chargeback is a governed disposition that may feed an external finance/accounting system, but the posting authority remains outside FinOps unless separately owned.

C3.26 remains owner of customer invoice/payment semantics.

Therefore:

`showback != chargeback != customer invoice`.

An organization may choose showback-only, chargeback, or hybrid treatment per policy. The semantic plane records which disposition applies and its producing allocation/rate revision.

## 12. Relative operational complexity as auditable economic evidence

**DECISION C3.27-D10 — relative operational complexity may inform internal cost/support economics only when derived from auditable architectural and operational facts; it is never a universal quality score or opaque pricing authority.**

The complexity representation is a **vector**, not a single enterprise health/risk scalar. Candidate dimensions include:

- number and criticality of external provider dependencies;
- authentication/authorization assurance requirements;
- MFA/session/credential lifecycle complexity;
- number of independently versioned integrations/protocols;
- data volume/growth/retention/restore obligations;
- workflow concurrency, retry, compensation and reconciliation surfaces;
- SLO class and on-call/escalation burden;
- offline/federated/Station autonomy requirements;
- deployment/runtime topology count and residual cohorts;
- observability cardinality/currentness requirements;
- security/privacy/compliance obligations;
- recovery/rollback/rebuild complexity;
- provider quota/rate-limit exposure;
- physical/peripheral integration breadth strictly within C2 boundaries.

Every dimension requires unit/scale definition, source facts, revision, evidence currentness and owner. Weights, if used for a bounded commercial/support model, are explicit effective-dated policy and produce a **derived relative complexity result**, not architecture truth.

Lines of code, developer intuition and AI sentiment are prohibited as sole authority. Two capabilities with the same aggregate could have radically different operational profiles; the vector remains inspectable.

## 13. Queueing, capacity and economic currentness

**DECISION C3.27-D11 — economic ingestion, normalization, allocation and reconciliation are queue networks whose backlog age and evidence horizon directly affect trustworthiness.**

The plane tracks, where applicable:

- arrival rate `λ`, service rate `μ`, utilization `ρ` only under a declared queueing model;
- queue depth and queue age;
- provider export delay and billing lag;
- late adjustment/credit/rebill arrival distribution;
- retry amplification and backoff;
- batch/pagination completeness;
- provider API quotas/rate limits;
- reconciliation throughput;
- forecast refresh cadence;
- month/period-close bursts;
- capacity headroom for provider outages and reconnect floods.

`low mean utilization != sustainable peak capacity` and `small queue depth != fresh economic truth`.

Economic dashboards must expose evidence as-of time/currentness and unresolved residuals rather than presenting stale values as current.

## 14. Provider substitution, coexistence and residual economic cohorts

**DECISION C3.27-D12 — provider substitution is a qualified coexistence/cutover process, not a rename of provider IDs.**

Substitution requires:

1. source-schema/support-vector qualification;
2. identity/dimension mapping;
3. overlapping evidence comparison when possible;
4. normalization equivalence tests;
5. cutover boundary with explicit effective time;
6. fencing of obsolete source authority;
7. drainage of residual late invoices, credits, adjustments, commitments, exports and stale mappings;
8. reconciliation and closure/disposition of residual economic cohorts.

`same SKU/service/category name != semantic equivalence`.

Remote provider-side economic actions, where FinOps consumes or requests them through another owner, preserve `APPLIED / NOT_APPLIED / PARTIAL / UNKNOWN`; `UNKNOWN -> reconcile-before-retry` when replay could create duplicated commitments, exports or policy effects.

## 15. Local, federated and Fleet economics

**DECISION C3.27-D13 — Fleet aggregation is a qualified projection over local/site/Station evidence and never overwrites local economic truth.**

A Fleet view carries population membership, revision vector, observation window, source completeness and unresolved/missing Station state. A disconnected Station may continue producing bounded local economic evidence if its source closure and clock/currentness requirements are qualified.

Reconnect does not equate to convergence. Late local evidence, duplicated uploads, changed mappings and provider-side corrections enter reconciliation before Fleet totals become complete.

`Fleet aggregate != local economic truth` and `aggregate != dimensional completeness`.

## 16. Brownfield / Legacy Mirroring assimilation

**DECISION C3.27-D14 — spreadsheets, provider portals, invoices, scripts, tag conventions, manual cost-center mappings, tickets and analyst procedures enter as evidence/candidate policy, not canonical economic truth by discovery.**

Brownfield assimilation records:

- source/procedure owner;
- observed fields and semantics;
- time/currentness;
- manual transformation steps;
- hidden assumptions;
- exceptions/residuals;
- reconciliation practice;
- evidence retention;
- approval/authority boundary.

Legacy Mirroring may reproduce producing calculations for comparison, but canonical adoption requires explicit qualification. AI may propose mappings and detect anomalies; it cannot silently promote them to authoritative allocation/rate/budget policy.

## 17. Privacy, security, governance and audit

**DECISION C3.27-D15 — FinOps observability must minimize sensitive data while preserving sufficient economic lineage and access accountability.**

Economic evidence may expose customer, tenant, employee, workload, project, account and usage relationships. Collection is purpose-bound and minimized; sensitive fields support redaction/pseudonymization where compatible with required reconciliation.

Access to cost, rate, forecast, commitment and allocation data is separately governed. Audit records policy revision, actor, evidence set, transformation, approval/disposition and export. FinOps cannot widen operational authorization merely because it owns economic visibility.

## 18. C1 Operability Elicitation Lens for FinOps

**DECISION C3.27-D16 — every FinOps deployment/flow must answer operational questions before it can be production-ready, even when its feature model is complete.**

Required elicitation includes:

- **Como saberemos que está funcionando?** Which source populations, reconciliation checks, conservation checks and freshness indicators prove healthy operation?
- **Como saberemos que está degradado?** Which lag, queue age, missing-population, residual/unallocated, correction-rate, quota or provider-health thresholds constitute degradation?
- **Quem é responsável?** Who owns each source, normalization policy, allocation policy, rate model, budget, forecast, commitment, reconciliation queue, alert and incident?
- **Que evidência precisamos?** Which invoices/exports/usage/currentness/provider metadata and producing revision vectors must be retained?
- **Qual estado pode permanecer UNKNOWN?** For how long may provider cost, late credits, mappings, commitment application, local uploads or allocation remain unresolved?
- **Qual perda/atraso é aceitável?** What economic reporting lag, missing-evidence tolerance and forecast staleness are acceptable per use case?
- **Como recuperar?** How are source reimports, rebuilds, historical replays, backup/restore and policy reconstruction performed?
- **Como reconciliar?** How are duplicate/late/corrected rows, changed tags, residual allocations, provider rebills and Fleet/local divergence resolved?
- **Como validar depois de mudança/deploy?** Which conservation, replay, shadow-comparison, historical reproduction and dashboard-currentness proofs must pass?

Additional questions cover SLO/SLA, expected throughput, peak/burst at billing close, pagination gaps, provider outage, rate limits, retry/idempotency, timeout/`UNKNOWN`, dependency health, data retention/archive, alert thresholds, escalation/on-call, maintenance windows, capacity headroom, cost of the FinOps pipeline itself, privacy-safe telemetry and incident response.

## 20. Observe / control / change authority

**DECISION C3.27-D18 — observing economic state, proposing optimization and executing changes are separate authority classes.**

FinOps may observe and derive economic evidence. It may propose optimization, commitment changes, provider migration, scaling or policy changes. Execution belongs to the owning capability/governance path unless explicitly delegated by a future authorized design.

An AI-generated savings recommendation cannot purchase a commitment, change authorization, scale a production workload, alter a provider contract or perform physical actuation merely because estimated savings are high.

## 23. External evidence portability notes

The target is provider-neutral but incorporates portable semantics challenged against mature external practice:

- FinOps Foundation Allocation separates direct/shared allocation and requires an explicit shared-cost strategy, supporting transparent allocation policy and responsibility views.
- FinOps Foundation distinguishes showback from chargeback and notes that chargeback depends on organizational accounting policy, supporting separate internal accountability dispositions.
- FOCUS 1.3/1.4 adds split-cost allocation, recency/completeness, commitment and invoice-reconciliation semantics, supporting qualified normalized evidence and late-correction/currentness handling across providers.
- AWS exposes multiple cost types (unblended, amortized, blended, net variants), cost categories and delayed activation of allocation tags, reinforcing that provider-native category/rate semantics and metadata currentness must be qualified rather than treated as one canonical cost number.

These are research/architecture challenge inputs only; none becomes SB authority by reference.
