## Evidence refresh

1. NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3, finalized April 2025, integrates incident response throughout cybersecurity risk management and treats detection, response and recovery as coordinated but distinct activities. Source: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final (rechecked 2026-09-05).
2. NIST's Incident Response project states that SP 800-61 Rev. 3 supersedes Rev. 2 and aligns response/recovery with CSF 2.0 rather than reducing incident handling to a single success state. Source: https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/incident-response (rechecked 2026-09-05).
3. CISA's `#StopRansomware Guide` requires offline/encrypted backups, regular availability/integrity testing, prioritized restoration, isolation, clean rebuild/reconnect, remediation of persistence/vulnerabilities and explicit criteria before declaring an incident over. Source: https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide (rechecked 2026-09-05).
4. Existing G2 Security research already separates prevention, detection, containment, eradication/rebuild, restoration, reprotection and validated return-to-service; it also catalogues recovery qualification, fencing/recovery competition, surviving external effects and degraded-authority conflict classes.

Portable conclusion remains unchanged: a recovery action can be locally successful while current security posture, business convergence, authority or residual-cohort safety remains unproven. Local success is evidence for its qualified subject, not universal proof of safe return-to-service.

## Techniques rotated for Full Pass 4

1. **security-claim horizon crossing** — move a valid threat/posture claim across trust, secret, provider, artifact, topology and Station revisions while holding the claim text constant;
2. **state-separation permutation** — independently permute prevention, detection, containment, rebuild, restoration, reprotection and business recovery to search for contradictory jointly-valid states;
3. **fencing acknowledgement inversion** — compare control-plane isolation success with delayed writer/session/credential/provider reachability and reconnect;
4. **degraded-mode fail-open/fail-closed braid** — combine continuity pressure, stale authority, unavailable policy/trust services and pre-authorized emergency leases;
5. **persistent-commitment recovery differential** — hold restore success constant while varying surviving payments, messages, approvals, allocations, human actions and physical effects;
6. **trust-secret-provider revision braid** — rotate trust, secret and binding epochs in different orders around containment, failover, restore and reconnect;
7. **compromised residual-cohort mutation** — vary offline Stations, cached credentials, delayed workers, old providers and restored nodes after apparent recovery closure;
8. **negative-evidence starvation** — shrink telemetry/scanning/provider evidence until a detector can no longer distinguish clean from unknown;
9. **backup-qualification substitution** — substitute integrity-tested backup, restorable backup, business-valid restore and secure/reprotected restore to detect invalid promotion between claims;
10. **presence-semantics security mutation** — vary `ABSENT`, `null`, empty, inherited/default and explicit delete across security configuration and provider adapters;
11. **human-runbook conflict braid** — compose individually valid security, continuity, data, trust, provider and business procedures whose next actions conflict;
12. **objective-frontier inversion** — vary availability, confidentiality, integrity, recovery-time, cost and compliance priorities without changing local component validity;
13. **AI/low-code confidence and authority delta** — compare source evidence/authority with generated recovery summary, plan and actuation scope;
