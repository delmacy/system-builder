## 2. Fresh evidence

FOCUS 1.4 is the current ratified FOCUS specification (June 4, 2026). It preserves distinct semantic roles for `BilledCost` and `EffectiveCost`: Billed Cost is invoice-basis cost, while Effective Cost is an accrual/economic recognition view that redistributes covering-charge cost across covered charges. FOCUS also makes billing-period status and correction semantics explicit: a closed period is final for normal processing, yet controlled corrections can still occur and can affect downstream allocation, chargeback and reporting.

OpenTelemetry Collector resilience guidance also confirms that queued retry and persistent WAL improve delivery resilience but do not prove complete telemetry: data can still be lost on queue exhaustion, retry expiry, disk exhaustion/failure or prolonged destination outage. Therefore runtime-local economic evidence, exported telemetry and fleet aggregate remain independently qualified claims.

Portable consequence: arithmetic validity or apparent fleet completeness never proves semantic comparability, source-population completeness, economic ownership or currentness.

## 4. Adversarial candidate screen

### A — local runtime cost evidence and provider billing are both valid but temporally incomparable

A client can correctly record local resource/workload usage while offline; the provider later emits finalized or corrected billed/effective cost under a different period/currentness cut. Fleet may join them by timestamp/build/capability and infer a unit cost that no semantic owner asserted.

**Disposition:** no new reusable family. Existing qualified-evidence/currentness, temporal/revision-vector, source-population, semantic-ownership and historical-reproduction patterns cover it.

**Detection candidate:** require explicit producing period/window, provider dataset instance/currentness, capability-use/build/deployment lineage and chosen economic basis before cross-source ratio/rollup. Mismatch is a signal, not a ConfirmedConflict.

### B — fleet-wide cost optimization uses partial telemetry as if it represented complete demand

Store-and-forward may be bounded and telemetry may be dropped, delayed or duplicated. A Fleet optimizer can therefore see a locally coherent but incomplete demand/cost picture and recommend capacity/provider changes that are numerically optimal for the observed subset.

**Disposition:** no new family. Existing `PARTIAL/UNKNOWN`, evidence currentness/completeness, objective conflict, authority non-amplification and resource/capacity families cover the activation conditions.

**Detection candidate:** bind aggregate decisions to coverage/completeness horizons and missing-cohort evidence; never translate missing export into zero usage/cost. Any future actuation requires separate explicit authority, client context, version target and rollback qualification.

### C — same canonical capability has incomparable economics across builds

Two builds can implement the same canonical capability but differ in provider, contract, runtime topology, batching, sampling, capacity model or formula/rate revision. Aggregating cost-per-invocation by `CanonicalCapabilityRef` alone can create a false benchmark.

**Disposition:** no new family. Existing semantic-equivalence/qualified-comparability, provider qualification, revision-vector and build/runtime lineage patterns cover it.

**Detection candidate:** define comparison cohort over capability use + build/release + deployment/runtime realization + provider/contract + economic profile. Capability-level rollup is derived only after equivalence/compatibility qualification.

### D — shared infrastructure produces correct total cost but ambiguous tenant attribution

A shared cluster/database/schema may have a valid provider invoice while per-client CPU, I/O, network, storage, DB or support burden is only partially observable or allocated by a heuristic. Exact total cost does not imply exact tenant economic ownership.

**Disposition:** no new family. Existing allocation-conservation, semantic ownership, shared-resource/noisy-neighbor, qualified-population and provider-native identity patterns cover it.

**Detection candidate:** retain shared-cost pool identity, allocation method/revision, coverage population and uncertainty; never promote an allocated share to provider-billed fact or canonical ownership without lineage.

### E — late correction after durable downstream adoption

A provider correction can be valid after showback/chargeback/budget/forecast or commitment decisions were validly made under earlier evidence. Historical overwrite and silent ignore are both unsafe.

**Disposition:** existing correction/supersession, downstream-adoption, compensation/recovery and historical-reproduction patterns; no new ID.

**Detection candidate:** producing snapshot + correction lineage + adoption/effect state + reversibility + current policy/owner evidence.

### F — objective conflict under AI/low-code optimization

An AI/low-code optimizer can reduce effective cost by lowering resilience headroom, changing provider/residency, exhausting commitment authority or concentrating load in ways that violate security/privacy/SLA/capacity policy even when all cost formulas and candidate actions are individually valid.

**Disposition:** existing objective/optimization, policy precedence, authority non-amplification and AI-composition conflict patterns.

**Detection candidate:** compare objective vector and policy/authority envelope before any future actuation. Observability may inform placement/provider selection only when the semantic graph declares compatibility and authority permits it; it must not rewrite workflow semantics.
